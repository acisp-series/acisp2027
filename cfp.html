Call for Papers

The 32nd Australasian Conference on Information Security and Privacy invites submissions of original research.

Submission site. Papers will be submitted via EasyChair. The submission site opens in due course — check the important dates below.

The 32nd Australasian Conference on Information Security and Privacy (ACISP 2027) invites submissions of original papers presenting novel research results in all aspects of information security and privacy. ACISP 2027 will take place in Melbourne, Australia. We welcome papers presenting theories, techniques, implementations, applications, and practical experiences on a variety of topics.

Systematization of Knowledge (SoK) papers

We solicit systematization of knowledge (SoK) papers that evaluate, systematize, and contextualize existing knowledge for topics of broad interest to the cybersecurity community. Examples include papers that provide an important new viewpoint on an established, major research area, support or challenge long-held beliefs in such an area with compelling evidence, or provide an extensive and realistic evaluation and comparison of competing approaches to solving specific security problems.

SoK papers must include the prefix “SoK:” in the title and will be reviewed by the full Program Committee. They will be accepted based on their treatment of existing work and value to the community.

Important dates

All deadlines are 23:59 AoE (UTC−12) unless stated otherwise.

Instructions for authors

ACISP 2027 is expected to run two rounds of submission (dates above):

  • The first round returns results of reject, major revision, or accept. Authors of major-revision papers are encouraged to submit updated manuscripts to round two.
  • The second round returns accept or reject only.

Note. Papers rejected in Round 1 may not be resubmitted in Round 2.

Submitted papers must not substantially overlap with papers that have been published or accepted for publication, or that are simultaneously in submission to a journal, conference, or workshop with published proceedings. All submissions should be properly anonymised with no author names, affiliations, acknowledgements, or obvious references. Papers not properly anonymised may be rejected without review.

All submitted papers will be evaluated on their merits — particularly importance to cryptography and cybersecurity, novelty, quality of execution, and presentation. For papers that might raise ethical concerns, authors are expected to convince reviewers that proper procedures (such as IRB approval or responsible disclosure) have been followed and due diligence has been made to minimise potential harm.

Preprints. Papers that have appeared on preprint platforms (e.g., arXiv) may be submitted, provided they otherwise comply with all ACISP submission guidelines (including anonymisation and dual-submission policies).

Paper format

Submissions must be written in English, as a PDF using Springer's LNCS package, no more than 20 pages including the bibliography, excluding well-marked appendices and supplementary material. Reviewers are not required to read the appendices or any supplementary material; papers should be intelligible and self-contained without them. Authors should not change any style of the LNCS format. Author instructions and LaTeX/Word templates for LNCS publications are available from Springer's conference proceedings guidelines. Submissions not following the required format may be rejected without review.

The proceedings will be published by Springer-Verlag as a volume of the Lecture Notes in Computer Science series. Authors of accepted papers must guarantee that at least one author will register, attend the conference, and present the paper. See the author information page for camera-ready instructions (published after acceptance).
